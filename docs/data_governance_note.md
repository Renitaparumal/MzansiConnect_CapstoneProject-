# Data Governance and Privacy Note

This capstone concerns subscriber churn at a South African mobile network operator. The supplied Phase 1 materials describe the data as synthetic, but the project should use production-grade safeguards and should not publish customer-level data. Before any real data is collected or used, the organization must confirm its authority and lawful purpose for processing, give required notices, and involve its information officer or privacy lead. This note is a project control plan, not legal advice.

Under POPIA, information about an identifiable living person is personal information. A direct subscriber key such as `customer_id` is identifying. Demographics such as `age`, `gender`, and `income_band`; contact or service geography such as `province` and `city_type`; and `signup_date` can identify a person directly in combination or through linkage. Plan and payment attributes, `monthly_spend`, churn status, network usage (`data_mb`, `voice_mins`, and `billing_month`), and support interactions (`ticket_id`, `ticket_category`, `created_at`, `resolved_at`, and `resolution_hours`) become personal information when linked to a subscriber. Ticket free text, if collected, may reveal additional sensitive details and should be excluded from this analysis unless demonstrably necessary and separately reviewed. The exact source schema still needs confirmation against the missing data dictionary.

Collect only attributes needed for the stated questions and define a prediction cutoff so that post-churn events are not reused as predictors. For external sharing, remove names, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, free-text notes, and direct identifiers. Replace internal join keys with random project tokens when record linkage is necessary; hashing alone is not anonymization if the key can be linked or reversed. Generalize age to defensible bands, dates to a coarser period, and geography to a sufficiently large area. Suppress small groups and inspect combinations (for example, age, province, and signup month) for re-identification risk. Share aggregate results wherever individual-level data is unnecessary, and document the residual risk and approval for each extract.

Store raw source files encrypted with restricted access, separate identifiers from analytical features, and maintain access and export logs. Give analysts the minimum permissions required; retention staff should receive only the approved scoring output and should not gain general access to raw records. Use an approved, written retention schedule: the supplied project brief proposes deleting or irreversibly anonymizing granular usage and support records no later than 36 months after account termination, subject to legal, contractual, and business requirements confirmed by the data owner. Delete working copies and backups under the same schedule, and retain only de-identified aggregates where justified.

One ethical risk is unfair or intrusive treatment caused by model error or bias. False positives can lead to unwanted marketing or unequal offers, while false negatives can deny timely service support. Evaluate errors and performance across appropriate age, income, plan, and regional groups; do not use sensitive or proxy attributes to deny service or impose worse terms. Keep a human accountable for decisions, offer a meaningful route to challenge or correct data, use proportionate retention interventions, and monitor outcomes after deployment. Suspend use if material group harms or data-quality failures are found.
